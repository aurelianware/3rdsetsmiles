Severe tooth pain almost always seems to arrive at the worst time — late at night or over a weekend, when the office is closed. The goal until you can be seen is simple: stay safe, reduce pain as much as reasonably possible, and know the signs that mean you shouldn't wait for morning.

This is general comfort guidance, not a prescription. Always follow medication labels and your own medical circumstances, and call a professional if you are unsure.

## First, Rule Out an Emergency

Before focusing on comfort, check for red flags that need urgent medical care rather than a wait-and-see approach:

- Swelling that affects **breathing or swallowing,** or is spreading toward the eye or neck
- **High fever** with facial swelling, or feeling severely unwell
- **Uncontrolled bleeding** or a significant injury

If any of these are present, go to the nearest emergency room or call 911. Otherwise, the steps below can help you get through until a dental visit.

## Steps That Usually Help

**1. Rinse gently with warm salt water.** A teaspoon of salt in a cup of warm water, swished and spit, can soothe irritated tissue and dislodge trapped debris.

**2. Use over-the-counter pain relief as directed.** Follow the label and your own health history. Do not exceed the recommended dose, and do not combine medications without guidance.

**3. Apply a cold compress to the outside of the cheek.** Twenty minutes on, twenty off, can reduce both pain and swelling.

**4. Keep your head slightly elevated.** Lying flat can increase throbbing; propping up with an extra pillow often helps at night.

**5. Gently floss around the sore tooth once.** If the pain is from something trapped between teeth, careful cleaning may bring quick relief.

**6. Avoid triggers.** Steer clear of very hot, cold, sweet, or hard foods, and try to chew on the other side.

## What to Avoid

- **Don't put aspirin directly on the gum or tooth** — it can burn the tissue.
- **Don't rely on repeated numbing gels** as a substitute for care; they wear off and can mask a worsening problem.
- **Don't apply heat** to a swollen area — heat can make swelling and infection worse.
- **Don't ignore swelling** that is getting bigger, even if the pain temporarily eases. A pain that suddenly disappears is not always good news; sometimes it means a nerve has died while an infection continues.

## Why After-Hours Pain Shouldn't Wait Too Long

Severe, lingering tooth pain often signals an infection or an inflamed nerve that will not resolve on its own. Home measures buy comfort and time — they do not treat the cause. The sooner you are evaluated, the more options you usually have, and the lower the chance of the problem spreading.

## When Morning Comes

Call a dentist as early as you can. Describe your symptoms honestly — when the pain started, what makes it better or worse, and any swelling — so the office can prioritize you appropriately. Many practices, including ours, keep time open for urgent problems.

## Bottom Line

For after-hours tooth pain without emergency red flags: rinse with salt water, use OTC pain relief per the label, apply cold to the cheek, elevate your head, and avoid the things that make it worse. Then get seen promptly. If swelling threatens breathing or swallowing, or you have a high fever, treat it as a medical emergency and go to the ER.
