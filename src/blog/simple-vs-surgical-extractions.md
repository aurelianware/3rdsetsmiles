If you've been told you need a tooth removed, you may have heard the words "simple" or "surgical" extraction and wondered what actually separates them. The short answer: it's about how the tooth is accessed and removed, not about how serious the situation is. Both are routine, both are done with your comfort in mind — they just differ in technique and, sometimes, in recovery.

## What a Simple Extraction Is

A simple extraction removes a tooth that is **fully visible above the gumline** and can be loosened and lifted out without cutting tissue or bone. It's typically used for:

- Teeth that are damaged by decay but still accessible
- Loose teeth from advanced gum disease
- Teeth being removed for orthodontic or prosthetic reasons

The area is thoroughly numbed, the tooth is gently loosened, and it's removed. Most people are surprised by how quick and comfortable it is.

## What a Surgical Extraction Is

A surgical extraction is used when a tooth **can't simply be lifted out** — because it's broken at the gumline, impacted (still partly or fully under the gum or bone), or has roots that require a more involved approach. It may include:

- A small incision to access the tooth
- Removing a little bone around the tooth, or sectioning the tooth into pieces to remove it gently
- A stitch or two to help the site heal

Wisdom teeth and teeth that have broken off are common reasons a removal becomes surgical. It's still a very routine procedure — "surgical" describes the technique, not a cause for alarm.

## How They Differ in the Appointment

| | Simple | Surgical |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tooth position | Visible, above the gum | Broken, impacted, or below the gum |
| Technique | Loosen and lift | May involve an incision, bone removal, or sectioning |
| Anesthesia | Local; sedation optional | Local; sedation more commonly offered |
| Stitches | Usually none | Sometimes |

## Recovery: What to Expect

Recovery from a **simple extraction** is usually straightforward — mild soreness for a day or two, managed with over-the-counter pain relief and a soft diet.

A **surgical extraction** can involve a bit more swelling and a slightly longer healing window, but it's still very manageable with good aftercare. In both cases, the first 24 hours matter most:

- Protect the blood clot in the socket — **avoid straws, spitting, and smoking.**
- Stick to **soft, cool foods** at first.
- Use **cold compresses** for swelling and take medications as directed.
- Keep the area clean with the gentle rinsing routine your dentist recommends.

Careful aftercare is also how you lower the risk of a painful complication called dry socket — worth reading about separately if you have an extraction coming up.

## Planning Ahead: Replacing the Tooth

If the extracted tooth isn't a wisdom tooth, it's worth discussing replacement — such as a [dental implant](/services/dental-implants/) — before or at the time of removal. In some cases the timing of extraction and implant placement can be coordinated, which your dentist can evaluate with imaging.

## Bottom Line

"Simple" and "surgical" describe *how* a tooth is removed, not how worried you should be. A visible tooth that lifts out is a simple extraction; a broken, impacted, or below-the-gum tooth calls for a surgical approach. Both are routine, both are done comfortably, and both heal well with good aftercare.
