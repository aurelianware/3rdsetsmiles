All-on-4 (also called All-on-X when more than four implants are used) can be life-changing for the right patient. It can also be the wrong fit for a patient who needs a different sequence of care first.

This guide explains the candidacy conversation in plain language so you can walk into a consultation prepared.

## The First Question Is Not "How Many Implants?"

A responsible full-arch consult starts with goals:

- Do you want fixed teeth that stay in place?
- Are you trying to solve pain, loose teeth, broken teeth, appearance, chewing function, or all of the above?
- Are you open to staged treatment if that is medically safer than a same-day approach?

The technical plan should follow your medical reality and goals, not the other way around.

## Core Factors That Influence Candidacy

### 1) Bone support and anatomy

A 3D scan (CBCT) helps the dentist evaluate available bone, sinus and nerve position, and implant angulation options. This determines whether a fixed full-arch plan is straightforward or if additional planning is needed.

### 2) Gum and infection status

Active infection, severe gum inflammation, or unresolved dental abscesses may require stabilization steps before final prosthetic planning.

### 3) Whole-health considerations

Medical history matters. Conditions and medications that affect healing, immunity, or bleeding risk can change timing and surgical protocol.

### 4) Bite force and habits

Night grinding, clenching, and bite pattern influence implant number, prosthetic material choices, and long-term maintenance strategy.

### 5) Daily maintenance readiness

Fixed full-arch teeth still require hygiene access and routine follow-up. Long-term success depends on maintenance, not surgery day alone.

## When an Alternative May Be Better First

Some patients are better served by an alternate path at first, such as:

- Stabilization with urgent treatment before definitive full-arch planning
- Implant-supported removable dentures as a lower-complexity step
- Phased extractions and healing before final implant placement

That does not mean full-arch treatment is off the table. It means treatment sequencing is being personalized.

## Questions to Ask at Your Consultation

Bring these questions with you:

1. What findings on my scan make me a good candidate or a borderline candidate?
2. Is my case better as one surgery or staged care, and why?
3. What are the main risks in my specific case?
4. What does short-term recovery usually look like for patients with similar starting conditions?
5. What maintenance schedule do you require after delivery?
6. If I am not a strong candidate today, what would improve candidacy?

## Cost Conversations: What Should Be Clear

A trustworthy treatment plan should explain what changes pricing, such as surgical complexity, number of implants, restorative materials, and whether staged treatment is necessary.

You should leave with written options, not pressure.

## Bottom Line

The best All-on-4 candidates are not just people with missing or failing teeth. They are patients whose anatomy, health profile, and treatment goals align with a fixed full-arch plan that can be delivered and maintained safely.

If you are comparing options, ask for a structured consultation, a written plan, and enough time to make a confident decision.
